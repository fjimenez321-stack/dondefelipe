The big picture

PHASE 1 · Foundations: matter and measurement
01

Matter and Measurement

~2 weeks

Learning goal: Describe, classify and measure matter using the language of science.

Core topics

  • What matter is: mass, volume and density
  • States of matter and phase changes (melting, boiling, sublimation, etc.)
  • Physical vs. chemical properties; physical vs. chemical changes
  • Pure substances (elements, compounds) vs. mixtures (homogeneous, heterogeneous)
  • Separation techniques: filtration, distillation, chromatography, evaporation
  • SI units, scientific notation, significant figures, unit conversion (dimensional analysis)

You should be able to

Why it comes first. Without measurement and a way to classify matter, nothing that follows has a foundation. Dimensional analysis, in particular, is the main tool you will use in Unit 7.

The Atom

Core topics

03

Electron Configuration

~2 weeks

Learning goal: Describe how electrons are arranged in an atom, and why that arrangement matters.

Core topics

  • Energy levels, sublevels (s, p, d, f) and orbitals
  • Aufbau principle, Hund's rule, Pauli exclusion principle
  • Valence electrons and Lewis dot symbols for atoms
  • Noble-gas (condensed) notation

You should be able to

Key unit. Electron configuration explains why atoms behave the way they do. It is the hinge of the whole course: the periodic table, bonding and reactivity all depend on it.

05

Chemical Bonding

~3 weeks

Learning goal: Explain how and why atoms join together, and how bond type shapes a substance's properties.

Core topics

  • The octet rule (and its exceptions)
  • Ionic, covalent (polar, nonpolar, coordinate) and metallic bonds
  • Lewis structures of molecules
  • Molecular geometry (VSEPR theory) and molecular polarity
  • Intermolecular forces: London dispersion, dipole–dipole, hydrogen bonding

You should be able to

Connection. Intermolecular forces explain boiling points and solubility, ideas you will return to in Unit 8 (solutions).

PHASE 3 · Change and quantity
07

Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry

~4 weeks

Learning goal: Represent chemical changes with equations and calculate the amounts involved.

Core topics

  • Chemical equations; law of conservation of mass (Lavoisier)
  • Balancing equations (inspection and algebraic methods)
  • Reaction types: synthesis, decomposition, single and double displacement, combustion
  • The mole, Avogadro's number, molar mass
  • Stoichiometric calculations: limiting reagent, excess reagent, percent yield
  • Percent composition; empirical and molecular formulas

You should be able to

The most important unit of the course. Almost all quantitative chemistry depends on mastering the mole. Do not move on until mole conversions feel automatic.

Acids, Bases and Redox (Introduction)

~3–4 weeks

Learning goal: Understand the two most common families of reactions and get a first look at rates and equilibrium.

Core topics

How to study this plan

Critical dependencies

If this unit is weak……these units will suffer
Unit 1 (measurement, unit conversion)Units 7, 8, 9
Unit 3 (electron configuration)Units 4, 5, 6
Unit 5 (bonding)Units 6, 8 and organic chemistry
Unit 7 (the mole)Units 8, 9, 10
Solve, don't just read.

Chemistry is learned by working problems. From Unit 7 onward, spend at least 60% of your study time on exercises.

Check before moving on.

At the end of each unit, try the "You should be able to" list without notes. If you can't do an item, review it first.

Space your practice.

Revisit earlier units briefly every week (especially nomenclature and mole conversions) instead of studying each topic only once.

Use videos actively.

Pause before the answer and try the problem yourself. Watching a solution feels like learning, but solving it is what builds the skill.

Link it to the lab and daily life.

Cooking, cleaning products, rust, batteries and breathing are all chemistry. Concrete examples make abstract ideas stick.

What's next? Organic chemistry is deliberately left out of this plan. It is a separate block that is best started once covalent bonding and molecular geometry (Unit 5) are solid.
